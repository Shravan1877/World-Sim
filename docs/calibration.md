### D36 arithmetic: f_min

Burn-in with the floor off (f_min = 0) and the other values above. Settled FOOD output (units/quarter):
DORNE 0.968, BRONTIA 3.661, CERES 28.462, FALKEN 10.125, AURELIA 0.270, EVERMERE 0.480 → **world 43.965**.
Population (millions): 150, 333.3, 200, 233.3, 100, 116.7 → **world 1133.33**.

    f_min = 0.7 × 43.965 / 1133.33 = 0.7 × 0.038793 = 0.027155  →  0.0272

**Problem:** food demand per person at the settled state (floor off) is DORNE 0.0496, BRONTIA 0.0264,
CERES 0.0367, FALKEN 0.0206, AURELIA 0.0227, EVERMERE 0.0250. With f_min = 0.0272 the floor binds in
BRONTIA, FALKEN, AURELIA and EVERMERE, not only in a food shock. A floor that binds nowhere at the
settled state would be 70% of the *lowest* country's food demand per person: 0.7 × 0.0206 = **0.0144**.
This needs an owner decision (D36 is open).

### Result: flow test still fails (stop rule reached)

Passes: money conserved to 1e-9, every invariant, unemployment < 25%, bond_market ≈ 0, treasuries
bounded (≤ 2.2 credits). Fails: four energy-importing countries settle at ~50% of their starting GDP;
prices range 0.19–2.15; stability falls to 0–30 in turns 1–3 and stays mostly below 40.

Why (diagnosis, seed 1):
1. **The burn-in does not settle.** After 8 turns energy importers still have 20–28% ENERGY shortages.
   During burn-in stability is held, so this is invisible; in turn 1 the `k_e` term (1.5 × 25 = −37)
   takes stability from 70 to ~27. BRONTIA's own ENERGY sector has zero revenue after burn-in (it died).
2. **Trade drains energy importers.** §6.4 imports only fill a gap (`M = D − S_dom`); exports never
   compete with home goods. Importers pay for food and energy every turn but earn only when another
   country is short of what they make. With one world currency and no exchange rate, their nominal
   economy shrinks until imports match exports (~50% of start), while DORNE and FALKEN hold up.
3. **Savings interest prints money** (`r/4 · H` paid by the bond market). With the old values the
   Taylor rule turned inflation into higher `r`, more money, more inflation (hyperinflation by turn 60).
   The price cap 0.05 tamed it in this pass; the loop is still there in the equations.
